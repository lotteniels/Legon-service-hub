# Graph algorithm efficiency

Generated by `GraphBenchmark`. Each figure is nanoseconds per run, taken as
the median of 21 batches of 500 runs, after 3000 warm-up runs. Batching is
necessary because a single run costs less than a microsecond, which is the same
order as the clock's own noise.

**Graph:** 58 locations, 117 roads, cost = TIME_ADJUSTED

## Median time in nanoseconds

| Algorithm | 10 loc | 20 loc | 30 loc | 40 loc | 50 loc | 58 loc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BFS | 1060 | 974 | 1544 | 2098 | 2956 | 3562 |
| DFS | 1592 | 1603 | 5182 | 3966 | 5347 | 7120 |
| Dijkstra | 1210 | 1100 | 1913 | 2731 | 3733 | 4270 |
| Prim | 1310 | 1290 | 3699 | 3047 | 4138 | 4715 |
| Kruskal | 1527 | 1317 | 2363 | 3543 | 4532 | 5476 |

## Shape of each slice

Slices are induced subgraphs grown outward from location 35, University Square,
in BFS order. Growing from the lowest ids instead leaves the smaller slices in
several disconnected pieces, and the timings then measure array allocation
rather than traversal.

| Locations | Roads | Components | Reachable from source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 14 | 1 | 10 |
| 20 | 33 | 1 | 20 |
| 30 | 54 | 1 | 30 |
| 40 | 79 | 1 | 40 |
| 50 | 98 | 1 | 50 |
| 58 | 117 | 1 | 58 |

Every slice is a single component, so all five algorithms cover the whole of
each one and the comparison is like-for-like. Kruskal still tracks road count
rather than location count, because sorting the roads is its dominant cost.

## Complexity, and what was measured

| Algorithm | Complexity | Dominant cost | At 58 locations |
|---|---|---|---|
| BFS | O(V + E) | one queue pass, no cost comparisons | 3562 ns |
| DFS | O(V + E) | one stack pass, no cost comparisons | 7120 ns |
| Dijkstra | O((V + E) log V) | heap operations on tentative costs | 4270 ns |
| Prim | O((V + E) log V) | heap operations on cheapest incident road | 4715 ns |
| Kruskal | O(E log E) | sorting all roads, then union-find | 5476 ns |

Dijkstra costs 1.2x what BFS does at full size, which is the price of
answering the cheapest-route question rather than the fewest-roads one.

## Caveat on these numbers

Fifty-eight locations is far too small for asymptotic behaviour to dominate.
Constant factors, allocation, and cache behaviour matter more here than the
log factor, so the ordering between two algorithms of the same complexity
class should not be read as meaningful, and neither should a curve that dips
between two adjacent sizes. Combined with the uneven connectivity above, these
are not a clean function of V. They are included to give the efficiency lab
real figures and to show the expected ordering at full size, not as a claim
about operational performance.
